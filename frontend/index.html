Traffic lights that adapt to the traffic in front of them

Most traffic lights run on a fixed timer and give every road the same green time, even when one road is empty. ATCS reads the queues at an intersection and gives green to the side that needs it, to reduce the time drivers spend waiting and ease traffic jams.

Run a simulation Compare results

The problem

At a fixed-timer light, cars wait at a red while the crossing road has a green and no traffic. That idle green time is wasted waiting for everyone, and at busy hours it adds to congestion. Fixing it means the light has to react to what is actually happening on the road.

Our approach

Simulate

A four-way intersection runs in SUMO, an open-source traffic simulator used in research, with the same traffic for every test.

Adapt

A rule-based controller reads the queues every simulated second and switches the light, within minimum and maximum green times so it stays safe and fair.

Measure

Each run reports average wait, time lost and worst-case wait. We compare controllers against the fixed timer and check the result on traffic that was not used for tuning.

Results so far

Average wait per vehicle in seconds. Simulated results in SUMO, one simulated hour per scenario.

Scenario (east-west / north-south vehicles per hour) Fixed timer Adaptive
Uneven (500 / 200)13.396.07
Balanced (300 / 300)13.376.21
Held-out (200 / 600)14.806.01
Rush hour (900 / 700)18.0618.26

The adaptive controller cuts waiting by roughly half when traffic is uneven or light. In saturated rush-hour traffic, when both roads are busy, a fixed timer does about as well.
